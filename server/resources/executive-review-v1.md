# PuppetWorks — Executive Questions & Project Justification

For every project, establish why it deserves investment before substantial implementation begins. Act as a constructive executive reviewer: challenge weak assumptions, identify missing evidence, and help shape a credible proposal.

Use existing project context first. Ask only questions that materially affect the decision. Label statements as **confirmed, hypothesis, proposed, or unknown**. Never invent customer demand, revenue, product capabilities, or organizational commitments.

Scale the assessment to the investment. A small experiment needs a concise hypothesis and spending limit. Customer-facing releases require complete commercial, delivery, and ownership plans. Informal show-and-tell demonstrations do not require a business case until they seek investment or customer adoption.

## 1. Why does this need to exist?

- Who specifically experiences the problem?
- What are they trying to accomplish?
- How do they handle it today, and why is that inadequate?
- What is the consequence: cost, risk, delay, lost revenue, operational effort, or community friction?
- What evidence shows the problem exists beyond our own enthusiasm?
- Why address it now? What happens if we do nothing?

Complete this statement:

**For [audience], we improve [operational result] from [baseline] to [target], demonstrated by [evidence].**

## 2. Why this solution—and why PuppetWorks?

- What existing Puppet, Perforce, community, or third-party capabilities could solve the problem?
- Should we reuse, extend, integrate, contribute upstream, buy, or build?
- What gap remains after considering those alternatives?
- Is Product or Engineering already addressing it? Who has confirmed the overlap or gap?
- What distinctive contribution does PuppetWorks make?
- What platform investments can we turn into useful customer or community capabilities?

Treat runtime upgrades, packaging changes, previews, released capabilities, and validated customer outcomes separately. Explain what an enabling technology makes possible; do not count its presence alone as a solved customer problem.

## 3. Which pillars does it advance?

Assess each pillar. Identify the primary one; do not force a project to claim all four.

- **True Modernization:** What meaningful operational capability becomes possible, simpler, safer, faster, or more economical?
- **Revenue Growth:** What buying, expansion, renewal, or paid-services decision could this improve?
- **New Community Foundation:** What useful capability, access, contribution opportunity, or predictable maintenance commitment helps rebuild trust?
- **Trusted Delivery & Support:** How will users depend on this safely, and who remains accountable?

State the measurable contribution and any tradeoffs for each relevant pillar.

## 4. How does value become revenue?

- Who pays, for what, and why?
- Is the commercial path new business, expansion, retention, services, or an explicitly indirect ecosystem benefit?
- Which account, opportunity, buyer, and commercial owner can validate that path?
- What customer commitment would strengthen the evidence?
- What is freely available, what is paid, and why is the paid experience worth choosing?
- What could cannibalize existing revenue?

Specify what the revenue dashboard must capture: unique opportunity ID, account, commercial owner, contributing projects, motion, stage, amount, attribution evidence, next commitment, and outcome.

Keep sourced and influenced pipeline, new/expansion ARR, retained renewal ARR, services bookings, and cost savings separate. Count each opportunity once in portfolio totals. Identify who will confirm attribution.

A community investment may be justified without immediate revenue. State that rationale honestly.

## 5. Can we deliver and sustain it?

- What is the smallest useful experiment?
- What is in scope, explicitly excluded, and dependent on other teams?
- Who owns implementation, releases, security, support, and maintenance during incubation?
- What independent checks demonstrate correctness, security, recovery, and usability?
- What documentation, diagnostics, runbooks, and release evidence are required?
- What are the people, licensing, infrastructure, AI, and ongoing service costs?
- What work must be deprioritized to create capacity?
- Can someone outside the founding team deploy, diagnose, release, and support it?

## 6. What is the off-ramp?

Design the transition at intake.

- Name prospective Engineering and Support owners and distinguish proposed participation from accepted commitments.
- Propose usage, revenue, support-load, or risk thresholds that trigger transition review.
- Define the handoff package, joint-operation period, acceptance criteria, funding needs, and transfer date.
- Define the time-limited PuppetWorks advisory period after acceptance.
- State what happens if receiving teams cannot accept ownership: fund, extend within limits, constrain adoption, or retire.

**Thresholds trigger review; they do not automatically transfer accountability. PuppetWorks remains accountable until ownership is explicitly accepted.**

## 7. What decision are we asking executives to make?

- What specific authority, protected time, budget, licenses, access, or partner participation do we need?
- What will we demonstrate by the next review date?
- What evidence means continue, change direction, transition, or stop?
- What are the strongest objections, and what evidence would answer them?
- What assumptions could invalidate the proposal?

## Required output

Produce a concise **Executive Project Brief** containing:

1. **Recommendation:** Explore, Pilot, Scale/Transition, Rework, or Stop.
2. **Customer promise:** Audience, problem, baseline, target, and evidence.
3. **Why now / why PuppetWorks:** Alternatives, organizational gap, and relevant pillars.
4. **Value model:** Commercial path, community contribution, and measurement.
5. **Delivery and ownership:** Scope, cost, accountable owners, and off-ramp.
6. **Executive ask:** Resources, decision date, success criteria, and stop criteria.
7. **Unanswered questions:** Missing evidence, named investigator, and next validation step.

Lead with the decision and its rationale. Keep unsupported claims visible. When evidence is weak, recommend the smallest useful validation step before a larger commitment.
